*Find your way around the Commonway System*

Your street was designed. How wide it is, how fast it feels, whether a child can cross it alone: someone made each of those choices, usually by following a standard.

The Commonway System gathers the best evidence on those choices into one system. It shows what good streets look like, from a single crosswalk to a whole street network, and cites a source for every recommendation. It is written for neighbors and engineers alike: plain language on top, technical detail underneath.

## Where do you want to start?

- **You have a street that needs fixing.** Start with [How Road Changes Actually Happen](/guide/make-it-happen/): who controls your road, and how to ask for a change.
- **You want to see what good looks like.** Browse all {patternPageCount} [Patterns](/patterns/), each describing what a street, intersection, or street element should be.
- **You want to fix a street that already exists.** Browse all {retrofitStrategyCount} [Retrofits](/retrofits/), which show how to move an existing street toward a pattern, such as a road diet or a signal-to-roundabout conversion.
- **You want to know why streets work the way they do.** Read [Core Concepts](/guide/how-the-transportation-ecosystem-fits-together/): who sets the rules, why the speed sign doesn't set the speed, and why added lanes fill up.
- **You want to know how the system is organized.** [Using the System](/guide/how-to-read-a-pattern/) explains pattern pages, IDs, density tiers, and street types.
- **You'd rather watch or listen.** The [Media](/media/) gallery collects videos, podcasts, and articles, each linked to the pages it supports.
- **You're checking the work.** [Reference](/references/) holds the technical layer beneath each pattern, and [Project Governance](/about/governance/) explains how disputes over evidence are settled.

## Why you can trust it

Every recommendation cites a source, and each source carries an [evidence tier](/about/governance/#how-disputes-over-evidence-are-resolved): Legal / regulatory, Evidence-based, or Precedent-based. A claim still waiting for a source is flagged in amber instead of left looking settled.

The Commonway System is compiled independently, not by an agency or firm, so it earns trust through citations rather than credentials. [Who's behind it](/about/commonway-system/).

It is maintained in the open. Anyone can [report a problem](/about/reporting/) or [submit a change](/about/contributing/), and every change is logged in [What's New](/about/release-history/).
