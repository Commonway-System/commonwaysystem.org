## Overview

This is the lightest-touch of the three protected bike lane retrofits. AASHTO's Green Book allows narrower vehicle lanes, down to 10 feet, in many contexts, and a four-lane arterial with turn lanes can often be restriped during routine maintenance to include a buffer and a physically separated bike lane without touching lane count or parking at all.<Citation index={1} />

## Where this leads: Target patterns

[Bike Highway / Greenway](/patterns/collectors/bike-highway-greenway/) (COL-BHG-04), [Default (Standard Collector)](/patterns/collectors/default/) (COL-DEF-01) with [Protected Bike Lane / Cycle Track](/patterns/elements/protected-bike-lane-cycle-track/) (ELM-PBL-37)

## Applicability and thresholds

Most applicable where existing lanes are wider than required for safe operation at the corridor's design speed, giving enough recoverable width to build a genuine physical buffer rather than a painted line alone.

## Evidence and effectiveness

FHWA identifies planned road diets and similar retrofits of higher-speed roads as prime opportunities to add separated bike lanes, since the same width analysis that supports a lane diet or road diet also identifies the space available for a buffer and protected lane.<Citation index={2} />

## Getting it built

Restriping-based, so it can move through the full construction spectrum starting from paint and flexible delineators at the demonstration or pilot stage, before committing to permanent curb or planter-based separation. See [Construction Methodology & The Build Spectrum](/guide/construction-methodology-spectrum/).

## Who can move this

Usually a city or county public works decision, achievable through routine restriping maintenance in many cases. See [Legal & Planning Mechanisms](/guide/legal-planning-mechanisms/).
