## Why this pattern exists

Organic/Irregular networks are a real, common existing condition, especially in historic cores worldwide, and the Patterns section’s role is descriptive documentation of what exists, not prescriptive endorsement of what to build. This pattern is documented for that reason: Commonway System does not recommend building new organic/irregular networks, but existing ones are a genuine, common reality that needs naming and description like any other topology.<Citation index={1} />

## Key considerations

- Irregular orientation and angularity often produce natural traffic calming through unpredictability and reduced sightlines, an incidental but real benefit of this topology
- Retrofitting modern accessibility and modal infrastructure into an organic network requires context-sensitive, block-by-block approaches rather than standard geometric assumptions that work on a regular grid
- Closely corresponds to Marshall’s A-type/Altstadt, typically found at a settlement’s historic core
