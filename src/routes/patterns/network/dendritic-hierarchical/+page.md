## Why this pattern exists

Dendritic/Hierarchical networks reduce through-traffic on local streets by design, which is genuinely valuable for neighborhood quiet and safety, but the same branching structure that filters traffic also reduces route choice and increases circuity for everyone, including people walking and biking. This pattern documents both the topology and its historical sub-stages together, since the four named stages are not separate topologies but points along one continuous progression toward heavier branching and fewer through-connections.<Citation index={1} /><Citation index={2} />

## Key considerations

- The four historical sub-stages (Fragmented Parallel, Warped Parallel, Loops and Lollipops, Lollipops on a Stick) represent increasing degrees of branching and decreasing route choice, not four separate patterns; later stages have progressively fewer access points, intersections, and lineal feet of street per unit area
- Pedestrian and bicycle cut-throughs at cul-de-sac heads meaningfully mitigate the circuity penalty for people not in cars, and should be a standard companion treatment rather than an afterthought
- Independent research has found residents of tree-like/dendritic networks drive measurably more than residents of grid networks, a consideration worth weighing against the traffic-reduction benefit this pattern provides
