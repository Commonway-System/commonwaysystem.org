The specific transition zone where a highway or rural road enters a town, village, or settlement edge, marked by deliberate physical cues (narrowing, street trees, lighting change, surface texture) that signal to drivers that the road’s character and behavior expectations have changed.
</PatternCard>

*Working assessment: this pattern’s status reflects a preliminary Commonway System judgment call, pending final review.*

## Why this pattern exists

Drivers respond to the road they perceive, not the posted number.<Citation index={1} /> A speed limit sign alone at a town line does little if the road geometry on both sides looks identical; self-explaining roadway research supports pairing regulatory changes with a visible physical change at the same point. This pattern is the specific, high-stakes case of Density Step-Down/Step-Up that occurs at a settlement’s edge, where the mismatch between rural approach speeds and in-town conditions (pedestrians, driveways, cross traffic) has real safety consequences.<Citation index={2} />

## Key considerations

- Gateway treatment should combine at least two sensory cues (visual narrowing plus a surface or planting change), not rely on signage alone
- Placement should give drivers enough sight distance to register and react to the change before reaching the area it protects
- Distinguish gateway treatment (a point-scale cue at the transition) from the broader step-down it belongs to (which may extend well past the gateway itself)
