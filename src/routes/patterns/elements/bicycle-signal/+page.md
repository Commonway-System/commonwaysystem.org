## Why this pattern exists

Where a bicycle facility crosses or interacts with an intersection in a way that a shared vehicle or pedestrian phase does not safely accommodate, a dedicated bicycle signal resolves the conflict explicitly rather than leaving cyclists to interpret a signal meant for another mode. This pattern exists to document where that dedicated treatment is warranted.

## Key considerations

- Most appropriate where a protected bike lane or cycle track meets an intersection with a conflicting turning movement that a shared phase cannot resolve safely
- Should be clearly distinguishable from the vehicle signal to avoid driver confusion about which indication applies to them
- Leading bicycle intervals (giving cyclists a head start before conflicting vehicle movements) are a common and effective use of this pattern
