## Why this pattern exists

Where a full barrier or curb is not warranted but vehicle access still needs to be controlled or blocked, a plaza edge, a bike facility entrance, a sidewalk protected from a nearby travel lane, bollards provide a lighter-weight, often more flexible solution. This pattern exists to document that distinct, smaller-scale protective and access-control role.

## Key considerations

- Spacing must allow pedestrian, wheelchair, and stroller passage while still restricting vehicle entry<Citation index={1} />
- Removable or retractable designs should be specified where emergency or service vehicle access is occasionally needed; where vehicle exclusion is the design driver, crash-rated bollards are spaced and tested to a recognized standard<Citation index={2} />
- Visibility (reflective marking, contrasting color) matters for both drivers and pedestrians; an unmarked bollard is itself a collision hazard
