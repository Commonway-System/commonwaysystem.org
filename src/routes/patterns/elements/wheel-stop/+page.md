## Why this pattern exists

Vehicles routinely pull farther into a parking space than intended, and without a physical stop, this can push a vehicle’s front end over a curb and into pedestrian space, into landscaping, or into an adjacent lane. This pattern exists to document this small but common protective element, distinct from the curb itself.<Citation index={1} />

## Key considerations

- Placement should account for typical vehicle overhang so the stop actually prevents encroachment rather than just marking the space’s nominal end
- Wheel stops in a path of pedestrian travel can themselves become a tripping hazard, and should be placed and colored to avoid this
- Not always appropriate: some contexts favor a full curb or landscaped buffer over a discrete wheel stop
