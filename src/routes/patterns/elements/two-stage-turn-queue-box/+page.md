## Why this pattern exists

A conventional vehicle-style left turn asks a cyclist to merge across one or more lanes of traffic to reach a turn position, a maneuver that becomes increasingly stressful and dangerous as traffic volume, speed, or lane count increases; a two-stage turn queue box removes that merge entirely by breaking the turn into two straight crossings. This pattern exists to document this specific, well-established alternative to the conventional turning movement.<Citation index={1} />

## Key considerations

- Box placement and orientation must align with the cyclist’s path in the second stage of the crossing, so the movement reads as intuitive rather than requiring guesswork
- Most valuable at intersections with multi-lane approaches or higher traffic volume, where a conventional merge-and-turn movement would be most stressful
- Signal timing at the intersection needs to accommodate the two-stage movement, since a cyclist queued in the box is waiting for the second phase of the crossing, not simply out of the way
