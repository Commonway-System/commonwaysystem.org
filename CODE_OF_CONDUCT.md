# Code of Conduct

Commonway System covers a topic, how streets should prioritize different modes of travel, that reliably attracts strong, non-evidence-based opinions in every direction. This project only works if disagreement stays focused on evidence rather than volume, repetition, or who argued longest.

## Expected

- Engage in good faith. Assume the other person is trying to make the guide more accurate, not trying to win an argument.
- Back a factual disagreement with a source, ranked by the same evidence tiers used throughout the guide: legal/regulatory, evidence-based, precedent-based. See [Governance](https://commonwaysystem.org/about/governance/) for how ties are resolved.
- Keep feedback about the work, not the person. "This citation doesn't support this claim" is welcome. Attacks on a contributor's competence, motives, or identity are not.
- Accept that a change not landing isn't a personal rejection. Commonway System isn't run by majority opinion, and a proposal without evidence at least as strong as what it would replace doesn't get made, regardless of who proposed it.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in an issue, a pull request, a commit message, or anywhere else connected to this project.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion: repeating a rejected claim without new evidence, derailing a thread, or trying to pressure a decision through volume of comments rather than argument.
- Publishing someone else's private information without their consent.
- Any conduct that would reasonably make someone unwilling to participate.

## Scope

This applies to every space connected to the project: the GitHub repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, commits), and anywhere else the project is officially represented.

## Enforcement

Commonway System is maintained by a single person, see [Governance](https://commonwaysystem.org/about/governance/). Reports of a violation can be opened as a GitHub issue. A report that needs to stay private should say so, and a private contact method will be worked out from there. Reports are read and acted on at the maintainer's discretion, which can include a warning, editing or removing content, or blocking someone from further participation.

Participants are expected to comply immediately when asked to stop a behavior.
